Process improvement

The first five questions to ask before redesigning a process

Start with the problem, the customer, and the evidence—not a preferred solution.

Start with a shared understanding

Operational improvement works best when the team agrees on the problem before discussing solutions. Ask who experiences the issue, when it happens, and what a better outcome would look like.

Make the current work visible

Follow the work from request to delivery. Record waiting time, handoffs, rework, and decision points. A simple process map often reveals more than an elaborate presentation.

Test a focused change

Choose one measurable improvement, assign an owner, and run a short pilot. Compare results to the baseline and speak with the people doing the work before expanding the change.

Build a repeatable routine

Review a small set of meaningful measures regularly. Use the meeting to make decisions, remove obstacles, and identify the next experiment.

Put the process review into practice

Choose a clear boundary

Define the request that starts the process and the outcome that ends it. Avoid mapping such a broad area that useful detail disappears.

Separate work from waiting

Record elapsed time as well as task duration. Long delays often sit between activities rather than inside them.

Run a small experiment

Test a focused change with an owner, a baseline, and a review date. Use the result to decide what to adjust next.
